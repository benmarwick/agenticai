# Instructions for Authors: How-to Articles in *Advances in Archaeological Practice*

Source: [Cambridge University Press — Author Instructions](https://www.cambridge.org/core/journals/advances-in-archaeological-practice/information/author-instructions/preparing-your-materials)

---

## What Is a How-to Article?

The How-to series publishes shorter, peer-reviewed, educational articles written by authors with expertise in a particular area of archaeological practice. The intent is to provide archaeologists with clear, concise, step-by-step guidelines for successfully completing tasks that are or are becoming common practice.

"Practice" is defined broadly; the series covers a wide range of subjects:

- **Technical topics** — for example, presenting the correct steps involved in collecting, preserving, and transporting a particular type of sample taken for laboratory analysis.
- **Management or administrative topics** — such as providing guidelines for assessing the significance of archaeological sites or developing an archaeological management plan.
- **Standardization or streamlining** — articles may provide suggestions for standardizing or streamlining common tasks.
- **Avoiding common mistakes** — articles could focus on avoiding common mistakes in practice.

---

## Article Length

The recommended length for a How-to Article is **3,500 words** (excluding cited references and the Abstract).

---

## Scope and Standards

- *Advances in Archaeological Practice* publishes original scholarly work on how archaeologists learn about the past, convey findings in the present, or manage resources for the future.
- Articles are short, succinct, and problem-oriented, offering tangible take-aways that can be applied quickly to the day-to-day work of archaeologists in academia, government, and private practice.
- Published articles are to represent *advances* in practice. Practices that are not original will only be considered if they are being applied in new ways or to new aspects of archaeology.
- Manuscripts do not need to illustrate "successes" — if they advance archaeological understanding, innovators sometimes make great strides even though the final result was not what was expected.

---

## Audience and Writing Style

- How-to manuscripts should be written for a **broad audience** and at a level that **undergraduate students** can comprehend.
- These manuscripts are to be **focused and short**.
- Define specialized terms clearly and **avoid excessive use of jargon**.
- Readers of *Advances in Archaeological Practice* come from a wide range of professional settings, so authors should avoid jargon readers cannot easily decipher. Technical terms in specialty areas should be defined.

---

## Restrictions

- Manuscripts must **not** cover proprietary approaches or technologies.
- Manuscripts should **not** present the appearance of advertising goods or services provided by the authors.
- Direction for using **multiple competitors' products** should be given to the greatest practical extent, if applicable.

---

## Peer Review

This journal uses a **double-anonymous** model of peer review. Submitted manuscripts are *not* to include a cover page with the names or contact information of the authors. Authors should be mindful of other places in the manuscript where they may disclose their identity: file names, footers, citations, in the data availability statement, acknowledgements, etc.

## Manuscript Preparation

### General Formatting

- Follow the [SAA Style Guide](https://saa.org/common/Uploaded%20files/saadocs/Publications/Style%20Guide/saa-style-guide_english_updated_2021_final08023c15928949dabd02faafb269fb1c.pdf) for all textual elements, including citations and references cited, number and date style, measurements, mathematical and statistical copy, radiometric ages and dates, use of quotations, spelling, italics, capitalization, hyphenation, abbreviations, accents and diacritical marks, contractions, serial commas, parentheses, and hyperlinks.

### Order of Elements in Initial Submission

Compile the main file in the following order, in a **single anonymized document file**:

1. English abstract (200 words or less) and English keywords (3–5 words)
2. Main text
3. Acknowledgments (including permit information and a description of any AI tools used)
4. Funding Statement
5. Data Availability Statement
6. Competing Interests statement
7. Notes
8. References Cited
9. Supplementary material caption(s) (if applicable)
10. Figure caption(s)
11. Table caption(s)

Figures and tables should be uploaded as **separate, numbered files**. Tables: `.doc(x)` and `.xls(x)` files are accepted; authors may prefer to submit large/complex tables in `.xls`.

### Abstract and Keywords

- The abstract should be **200 words or less**.
- Submit **3–5 keywords**.
- Spanish abstracts and keywords for manuscripts written in English are not required at submission but will be required if the manuscript is accepted for publication.
- Abstracts and keywords can also be accepted in other languages most appropriate to the region and people with whom you work. Professional foreign language translations are the responsibility of the authors; computer-generated translations are not accepted.

### Acknowledgments

- Insert at the end of the text, using a tertiary heading **Acknowledgments**, placed flush left, immediately preceding the Funding Statement.
- Should be **brief**. Verbose acknowledgments will be edited prior to publication.
- Phrases such as "all errors are the sole responsibility of the author" should be omitted.
- Must contain a statement regarding the permits needed for the work described (permit number(s), year(s), and permitting agency). If no permit was required, this should be noted.
- Do not thank editors in the published acknowledgments.

### Funding Statement

Insert following the Acknowledgments, using a tertiary heading **Funding Statement**, placed flush left, immediately preceding the Data Availability Statement.

- For single agency grants: "This work was supported by the [Funding Agency] under Grant [number xxxx]."
- For multiple agency grants: "This work was supported by the [Funding Agency 1] under Grant [number xxxx]; [Funding Agency 2] under Grant [number xxxx]; and [Funding Agency 3] under Grant [number xxxx]."
- If no specific funding: "This research received no specific grant funding from any funding agency, commercial or not-for-profit sectors."

### Data Availability Statement

The DAS should communicate to readers where the data are available or where the objects from which the data were derived can be obtained.

- Manuscripts that do not use original data should state: "No original data were used."
- Data, both physical and digital, should be curated at a suitable institution.
- A statement that data may be obtained by contacting the author is generally **not** sufficient.
- Supplementary material is not an acceptable place to provide raw data. Raw data should be deposited in a repository and referenced in the text by a DOI (e.g., tdar.org, zenodo.org, osf.io, etc.).

### Competing Interests

All authors must include a competing interests declaration in their final manuscript.

- Example: "Competing interests: Author 1 is employed at organization A. Author 2 is on the Board of company B and is a member of organization C. Author 3 has received grants from company D."
- If no competing interests: "Competing interests: The author(s) declare none."

### Notes

- Used sparingly to provide absolutely essential additional information or clarification only when inclusion in the text would prove disruptive.
- Begin on a new page before the References Cited section, under the primary heading **Notes**.
- Footnotes are not allowed.
- List each note as paragraph style, beginning with the appropriate number.
- If a utility was used to compile hyperlinked endnotes, it must be disabled and the manuscript submitted with plain text formatting.

### In-Text Reference Citations

- In-text year citations always immediately follow the author name(s).
- Use parentheses in ordinary format; when citations appear in textual material set off in parentheses, convert parentheses to brackets.
- Include page numbers for citations to books or monographs.
- Always provide page numbers for quotations, definitions, paraphrasing, and other use of exact terms regardless of the source.
- A CSL file for Zotero/Mendeley is available on [GitHub](https://github.com/citation-style-language/styles/blob/master/society-for-american-archaeology.csl) and [Zotero](https://www.zotero.org).

### References Cited

- All references cited in the text must appear in the References Cited list (except personal communications and primary-source materials), and all entries in the list must be cited in the text.
- Entries are flush left. Order: **author(s)/editor(s), date, title (and subtitle), publisher, location of publisher**.
- Alphabetize by last names of authors. Use complete first names and middle initials as they appear on the title page.
- Two or more works by the same author(s) are listed chronologically; same year entries are differentiated by lowercase letters (e.g., 1991a, 1991b).
- Use **headline-style capitalization** for all English language titles.
- If a bibliographic compiler was used, convert output to plain text before submitting.

### Figures, Artwork, and Graphics

- Articles should be **well illustrated** with color photographs, graphs, maps, and illustrations.
- All digital photographs and graphics must be at least **300 dpi in resolution at 7 inches wide** (4 megapixels).
- Written permission must be obtained from the copyright holder (usually the photographer) and from each individual depicted in a recognizable fashion.
- Obtain permission to use images of people who can be recognized early, as this can be time consuming.
- See the [Cambridge Journals Artwork Guide](https://www.cambridge.org/core/services/authors/journals/journals-artwork-guide) for detailed guidance.

### Tables

- Use tabular presentation sparingly. Short data can often be included in the text.
- Avoid abbreviations and acronyms in tables; if unavoidable, spell them out in accompanying notes.
- Each table should have a title.
- Editors may require long tables to be published online as supplementary material.
- Submit data tables in `.xls(x)`, `.doc(x)`, or similar commonly used formats. Do **not** submit as image files.
- See Chapter 3 of the *Chicago Manual of Style*, 17th edition, and the SAA Style Guide.

### Accessibility

- All articles must meet [WCAG 2.1 Level AA](https://www.w3.org/TR/WCAG21/) standards.
- Consider colour use, contrast, and text clarity when preparing images and tables.
- Provide image descriptions ("alt-text") for all figures and descriptions for tables and multimedia content.
- See [Preparing Accessible Materials](https://www.cambridge.org/core/services/authors/preparing-accessible-materials) for detailed guidance.
- Diagrams, graphs, maps, boxes, graphical diagrams, and code snippets must be labelled and numbered as "Figures".

### Digital Material

Digital materials (3D models, animations, video, software applications) can be included. Videos cannot be uploaded to Editorial Manager — contact the editor for transfer information.

### Supplementary Materials

- Non-essential but relevant material may be submitted as supplementary materials, published online alongside the article.
- Types may include appendices, additional tables or figures, datasets, videos, and sound files.
- Supplementary materials will be published with the same metadata as the parent article and are a formal part of the academic record.
- They will not be typeset or copyedited — supply them exactly as they are to appear online.
- Place the supplementary materials list directly before the Reference list, numbered sequentially.

## Post-Acceptance Requirements

If an article is accepted for publication, authors will be asked to submit final files in the following order and with the following additional elements:

1. Title page and author list with Corresponding Author designation, full contact details, and affiliations (including country)
2. Second-language abstract(s) (200 words or less) and second-language keywords (3–5 words)
3. Any additional non-anonymized materials

## Policy on Prior Publication

- Manuscripts submitted should not be under consideration, accepted for publication, or in press within a different journal, book, or similar entity, unless explicit permission has been sought from all entities involved.
- Deposition of a preprint on the author's personal website, in an institutional repository, or in a preprint archive is **not** viewed as prior or duplicate publication.
- Follow the Cambridge University Press [Preprint Policy](https://www.cambridge.org/core/services/open-access-policies/open-access-journals/preprint-policy).

## ORCID

All corresponding authors must identify themselves using [ORCID](https://orcid.org/register) when submitting a manuscript.

## Use of AI Tools

- AI tools (e.g., ChatGPT) must **not** be listed as authors.
- All use of AI-generated content must comply with the plagiarism policy and best practices regarding citation and acknowledgement.
- Declare use of AI tools if used to generate text, images, translation, or to analyse/extract insights from data.
- Declaration should appear in the acknowledgments (for text generation), methods section (for data analysis), or figure captions (for visual content).
- Include: tool name and version, date(s) of use, access information, full description of use, and appropriate citations to third-party material used.

## Themed Issues

- AAP aims to publish two themed issues per year, bringing together 8–12 papers on a shared topic regarding contemporary archaeological practice.
- Themed issues should have a strong problem orientation and bring together a diverse group of authors.
- See [past themed issues](https://www.cambridge.org/core/journals/advances-in-archaeological-practice/special-issues) and [proposal guidance](https://www.cambridge.org/core/journals/advances-in-archaeological-practice/themed-issues/submit-a-themed-issue-proposal).
