# Human at the Helm vs Human in the Loop

**Date:** 2026-09-16 | **Purpose:** framing section for the AAP how-to article on agentic AI for archaeological data analysis in R

All citations below were verified against Crossref/Semantic Scholar on 2026-09-15.

---

## Summary (2–3 sentences)

Human-in-the-loop (HITL) design inserts a person at discrete decision points in an otherwise automated workflow, where the researcher reviews or corrects the machine's output rather than directing the work itself (Zanzotto, 2019; Taylor, 2023). The "human at the helm" framing goes further: the researcher sets the goals, steers the analysis, interprets the results, and retains responsibility throughout, so the AI acts as an instrument rather than the driver (Monakhov, 2026; Jonker et al., 2024; Hao et al., 2025). We adopt the latter position for research with AI, because scientific judgment, interpretation, and accountability cannot be delegated to a system that is indifferent to the truth of its outputs (Hicks et al., 2024).

## Comparison Table

| Dimension | Human in the Loop (HITL) | Human at the Helm |
|-----------|--------------------------|-------------------|
| **Locus of authority** | AI drives the workflow; the human approves at checkpoints | Human directs the workflow; AI executes under direction |
| **Human role** | Intermittent reviewer, validator, or corrector | Continuous director and final decision-maker |
| **When humans act** | At predefined intervention points (approval gates, error correction) | Throughout the process, including goal definition and result interpretation |
| **Control logic** | Oversight of individual AI outputs (exception handling) | Steering the entire trajectory of the research task |
| **Accountability** | Distributed between system and operator; often ambiguous in practice | Retained by the researcher from start to finish |
| **Epistemic stance** | Machine output is assumed usable until flagged | Machine output is provisional until the researcher has understood and endorsed it |
| **Relation to research integrity** | Can automate reasoning while the human only reacts | Preserves human agency; the researcher owns the reasoning and the result |
| **Scientific framing** | HITL is a system-design pattern common in ML and RL (Zanzotto, 2019; Taylor, 2023) | Extends meaningful human control to research contexts (Jonker et al., 2024; Beck & Burri, 2024) |
| **Regulatory echo** | Absent in law; legal discourse instead requires oversight, not mere presence in a loop (Beck & Burri, 2024) | Aligns with "human oversight" and control requirements in AI regulation (Beck & Burri, 2024; Monakhov, 2026) |
| **Risk profile** | Risk of automation bias: the human rubber-stamps machine output | Risk is reduced by continuous human direction, though the human must stay engaged |
| **Definition of collaboration** | Human and AI as separable stages in a pipeline | Human and AI as a joint sensemaking process; humans add value AI cannot (Hao et al., 2025) |
